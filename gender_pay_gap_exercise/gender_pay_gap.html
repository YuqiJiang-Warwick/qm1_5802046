5. Hypothesis Testing

I use a two-sided one-sample t-test to examine whether the mean gender pay gap across reporting employers is significantly different from zero.

Let \(\mu\) denote the population mean of employer-level gender pay gaps.

H0: The mean gender pay gap is zero (\(\mu = 0\)).

H1: The mean gender pay gap is not zero (\(\mu \neq 0\)).

t.test(x=pay_gap$DiffMeanHourlyPercent, mu=0)

6. Results and Interpretation

The mean hourly gender pay gap across UK reporting employers was 11.50%, indicating that men earned more per hour than women on average within employers. A two-sided one-sample t-test showed that the mean gap was statistically significant, t(11,186) = 76.06, p < .001.

Note: In the lecture example, the p-value was reported as an exact value (p = .032). However, my t-test produced an extremely small p-value (p < 2.2e-16). I reported it as p < .001 instead. Is this the preferred reporting convention when the p-value is extremely small?
